## How the result matches the target

The target fixes $`n\ge3`$ and $`L>0`$ and asks whether the regular $`n`$-gon maximizes $`\sigma_1`$ among convex polygons with at most $`n`$ sides and perimeter $`L`$, uniquely up to congruence. For $`n=3`$ these polygons are exactly the triangles of perimeter $`L`$. Since $`\sigma_1`$ scales like $`L^{-1}`$, comparing at fixed perimeter is equivalent to comparing the scale-invariant product $`\sigma_1\mathop{\mathrm{Per}}`$. The theorem therefore proves exactly the $`n=3`$ case of the statement, including uniqueness of the maximizer up to congruence. The eigenvalue is the one defined weakly in the target: the infimum of the Rayleigh quotient over $`H^1`$ functions with zero boundary mean.
