## Target comparison

The [pinned statement](statement.md) is existential in diffusion, inhibitory coupling, delay, both voltage thresholds and period. It permits real thresholds with only the ordering constraint. Theorem 1.1 takes diffusion one and constructs a local nonconstant branch with negative thresholds, shifted together with coupling. It does not establish periodicity at prescribed thresholds or at the positive thresholds studied by some cited papers.

All target requirements are addressed: nonnegative unit-mass density, nonconstant firing rate, periodicity at a fixed finite delay, continuity in voltage, classical regularity away from reset, the correct reset derivative jump, zero lower probability flux, uniform second moment and a definition for all real times.

## Argument checked

1. **Stationary profile and signs.** Integrating the displayed positive Gaussian reset profile gives the mean hitting time m0 and normalized rate N0. Its upper derivative is minus N0 and its reset derivative jump is minus N0. The piecewise operator therefore conserves mass; the distributional second derivative and reset source cancel with the correct signs.
2. **Operator domain and resolvent.** The Gaussian conjugation gives the Dirichlet half-line harmonic oscillator, with absorbing spectrum minus 1, minus 3, and so on. The Green point source has derivative jump minus 1. Taking the firing trace in the absorbing equation gives the rank-one reset formula. A boundary elliptic estimate next to zero controls the trace in the graph norm; the absorbing resolvent then controls the full piecewise oscillator domain. Closedness, density and compactness follow without assuming a nonlinear delay semigroup theorem.
3. **No imaginary reset resonance.** The OU first hitting time is a time-changed Brownian first-passage time. Its explicit density is positive at every positive time, so its characteristic function has modulus strictly below one away from zero. Its finite positive mean makes the zero pole simple. Integrating the resolvent equation identifies the residue as the stationary profile times mass, hence removes it on the zero-mass subspace.
4. **Large-frequency estimates.** The Mehler kernel and its reflected Dirichlet version give spectral-mass bounds of orders one half at an interior point and three halves at the boundary derivative. Dyadic spectral sums give the point-source resolvent norm of order minus three quarters and the firing-trace resolvent norm of order minus one quarter. Their product has order minus one. Thus the reset resolvent has the required imaginary-axis estimate, including its graph norm.
5. **Periodic spaces.** The two resolvent bounds supply one time derivative and one graph norm. The trace interpolation exponents one quarter and three quarters give time regularity 5/4 for the firing trace, making its map compact into H^1 in time. Derivative interpolation gives regularity 3/2 for the voltage derivative. Their products lie in the forcing space; the forcing has zero mass. The delay phase is fixed, so differentiation of a variable translation is unnecessary.
6. **Response calculation.** Integration against the OU hitting function and the stationary weak identity give the exact response formula, including the factor lambda+1. The boundary derivative is a gamma ratio. Its first correction is minus 1/(4 lambda); division by lambda+1 adds minus 1/lambda, giving the coefficient minus 5/4. The remote-reset terms and their frequency derivatives are smaller than every inverse power by integration by parts in the flat hitting-time density. A sector-uniform analytic remainder admits differentiated asymptotics by Cauchy estimates. Consequently the phase derivative is nonzero for all sufficiently large frequencies. Differentiating the stationary firing rate under a constant input gives K(0)>0 with the correct sign.
7. **All harmonics and reduction.** The asymptotic modulus bounds apply uniformly to the infinite family of harmonics, with upper/lower ratio 1.1/0.9 divided by the square root of n, strictly below one for every n at least two. Only the first conjugate harmonics are critical. The trace perturbation is compact, the real kernel and cokernel have dimension two, and the specified complement is transverse to the kernel. The two reduced Jacobian columns are minus 1/b0 and minus i K'/K. Their real-linear independence follows from the nonzero phase derivative. Ordinary Banach implicit-function arguments yield the parameter branch. The complement forces the first firing harmonic to equal the nonzero branch parameter exactly.
8. **Positivity.** Hilbert-norm smallness is not used to assert pointwise positivity. It instead ensures positive reset rate and small centered input. Testing the transformed equation against its negative part includes a nonpositive reset contribution. The Dirichlet oscillator lower bound and Young's inequality give a strictly dissipative negative-part estimate. Integrating over one period forces the negative part to vanish. Graph and time regularity justify this test by cutoff and approximation.
9. **Regularity and reconstruction.** Sobolev products and the periodic inverse bootstrap time regularity by one quarter at each step; the local differential equation then bootstraps spatial regularity away from reset. The Gaussian weight gives the uniform second moment. The transformed piecewise H^2 domain bounds the profile and first derivative in the lower tail, establishing the zero-flux limit. The fixed voltage translation restores the original delayed PDE, flux identity and reset, without adding an external forcing. Periodicity extends the solution to all real times.

No mathematical obstruction was found in this pre-submission check. The resolvent-domain estimates, differentiated response asymptotics, nonlinear reduction and negative-part test merit particular attention in independent review because they carry the full-PDE claim.
