Mathematical checks
1. The simplex boundary-rank proof uses a cone vertex to exhibit an
   independent spanning family of boundaries. Its coefficients are
   nonzero signs over any field, including characteristic two. The
   rank is choose(r-1,k+1), with the stated zero convention.

2. For the ultrametric pullbacks, all complexes at thresholds 0,1,2
   are disjoint unions of full simplices. Their positive-degree
   homology therefore vanishes. Every positive-degree bar is diagonal,
   and cumulative deaths equal boundary rank. Differences of these
   ranks give the claimed multiplicities without a tie-order choice.

3. For the original four-point spaces the ranks yield X's barcode
   {(1,1),(2,2),(2,2)} and Y's and Z's common barcode {(2,2)^3}.
   The two surjections in the note are onto. The five-vertex pullbacks
   have identical component-size lists at every threshold, hence
   identical degree-one barcodes and zero matching cost. The identity
   four-point comparison gives D_1(Y,Z)=0.

4. For arbitrary positive fiber sizes a,b,c,d over X, the count of
   (1,1) bars is exactly ab+ac+bc-2, at least one. The contribution
   from d cancels. This proof covers every finite common set, without
   imposing a fiber-size or cardinality bound. Every Z-pullback has
   diagonal bars at only 0 or 2, so every actual bijection must move
   an X-bar at 1 by cost one. Identical common-set cardinality gives
   equal total numbers of bars. Original-space matching attains one.

5. For k>=1, n=k+3 gives X cluster sizes k+2,1 and Y sizes k+1,2.
   Their original degree-k multiplicities and the (k+4)-vertex witness
   follow from the same rank formula. A size-two duplicated fiber
   creates no degree-k bars at zero. At level one both witness
   pullbacks have rank one, and at level two rank choose(k+3,k+1).

6. The all-fiber inequality is valid: the disjoint union of the sets
   of sizes m_i-1 and an additional set R of size k+1 has size M-1.
   Subtracting subsets contained in one fiber-set leaves the subset
   R, so at least one (1,1) bar remains for every multiplicity vector.
   This is an analytic universal proof, not an inference from tests.

7. The diameter-bar lemma holds for arbitrary finite pseudometrics
   with at least k+2 distinct vertices. A (k+1)-simplex containing a
   diameter-realizing edge has a boundary with a nonzero coefficient
   on a k-face entering exactly at the diameter. It is a new cycle
   outside the earlier chain space. Thus the cumulative cycle-space
   dimension, equivalently the count of degree-k births, increases
   there. Since the final complex is a full simplex, the newly born
   bar dies at that same diameter. This also covers diameter zero.

8. If the equal original cardinality n<=k+1, empty original barcodes
   give distance zero under the repository's explicit convention.
   If n=k+2, the sole original degree-k pair is the diameter bar.
   All pullbacks retain their target diameter and contain a diameter
   bar; matching the larger diameter bar costs at least the diameter
   difference because every endpoint in the other barcode is bounded
   by its diameter. This proves equality with the diameter difference
   and the triangle inequality on that cardinality class. Together
   with the construction, it establishes the stated equal-cardinality
   minimum k+3. It does not claim minimum total cardinality for triples
   of unequal sizes.

9. The correspondence restriction is met: identity pairs plus (n,1)
   are distinct, onto pairs realizing the required duplication of
   X's singleton and one member of Y's large cluster. The other
   comparisons use bijections. Nontransitivity of zero distance,
   absence of a finite multiplicative triangle constant, and absence
   of a universal additive defect bound follow by the stated scaling.
