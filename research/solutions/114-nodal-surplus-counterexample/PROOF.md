## Target and graph sequence

The [frozen AIM 114 statement](statement.md) asks both for a universal Gaussian limit and universal linear variance bounds. The construction below disproves the Gaussian assertion. It has linear variance itself and leaves the universal variance assertion unresolved.

For each integer $`m\ge3`$, use vertices $`u,v,w_1,\ldots,w_m,t`$, two parallel edges from $`u`$ to each $`w_i`$, one edge from each $`w_i`$ to $`v`$, and a pendant edge $`ut`$. There are $`3m+1`$ edges and $`m+3`$ vertices, so $`\beta_m=2m-1`$. The graph is connected and loop-free. The vertex degrees are $`2m+1,m,3,\ldots,3,1`$, none equal to two.

Let $`p_j`$ denote the $`j`$th prime. Assign the three core edges in module order the lengths $`\ell_j=\sqrt{p_j}`$ for $`1\le j\le3m`$, and assign the pendant length $`\ell_0=m^6\sqrt{p_{3m+1}}`$. All lengths are positive and rationally independent: in the multiquadratic extension generated by the prime square roots, an automorphism changing just one square root's sign isolates its coefficient in any rational linear relation. Integer multiplication of the last root preserves this independence.

The operator is the standard Kirchhoff Laplacian, with the generic-index convention in the target. Write $`S_m`$ for its spectral-frequency surplus law. The frequency limit is taken for each fixed graph before $`m\to\infty`$.

## Reduction to independent core phases

The external spectral input is [Alon–Band–Berkolaiko, arXiv:2106.06096v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.06096v2), Proposition 6.1, equation (6.7), and Lemma 6.7. For a loop-free graph these results give symmetric edge laws $`W_e`$ and normalized measures on generic secular phases with

```math
\mathcal L(S_m)=\sum_e\frac{\ell_e}{\sum_f\ell_f}W_e.
```

Under the measure for an edge, dropping that edge's phase gives uniform product measure on the remaining phase torus. For the pendant edge this makes all $`3m`$ core phases independent and uniform on $`[0,2\pi)`$. Denote its surplus law by $`\widetilde S_m`$.

For a module define

```math
x_{ij}=\cot\theta_{ij},\quad c_{ij}=\csc\theta_{ij},\quad
a_i=x_{i1}+x_{i2}+x_{i3},\quad p_i=c_{i1}+c_{i2},\quad q_i=c_{i3},
```

```math
\xi_i=\left(\frac{p_iq_i}{a_i},-x_{i3}+\frac{q_i^2}{a_i}\right),\qquad
(U_m,T_m)=\sum_{i=1}^m\xi_i,\qquad R_m=-U_m/T_m.
```

For $`r\ne0`$ put

```math
h_i(r)=\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}(a_i)}2
\left[1-\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}(p_i+rq_i)
\left(\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}c_{i1}+\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}c_{i2}
+\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}(rq_i)\right)\right].
```

The key exact identity, valid almost everywhere, is

```math
\widetilde S_m-\frac{2m-1}{2}
=\sum_{i=1}^m h_i(R_m)-\frac12\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}T_m. \tag{1}
```

Here is the spectral and algebraic justification. An edge of phase $`\theta`$ contributes the outgoing-derivative matrix

```math
\begin{pmatrix}-\cot\theta&\csc\theta\\\csc\theta&-\cot\theta\end{pmatrix}
```

to the vertex matrix $`M`$. At a generic eigenvalue $`k^2=\lambda_n>0`$ with no edge sine zero, a vertex vector $`F`$ satisfies $`MF=0`$. Set

```math
D=\sum_e\lfloor k\ell_e/\pi\rfloor,\qquad
Q=\sum_{e=ab}\mathbf1_{\{F_aF_b\csc(k\ell_e)<0\}}.
```

The edgewise zero count is $`D+Q`$. Decompose the quadratic form of the Laplacian minus $`k^2`$ into vertex-zero functions and Helmholtz extensions of vertex data. Integration by parts makes these subspaces orthogonal for the form. Their negative indices are respectively $`D`$ and $`n_+(M)`$, because the extension form is $`-kF^TMF`$. The min–max characterization then gives $`n-1=D+n_+(M)`$. Consequently the surplus is $`Q-n_+(M)`$. If $`F_u\ne0`$, replacing the $`u`$th basis vector by $`F`$ shows $`M`$ congruent to $`0\oplus M_{\setminus u}`$.

Normalize $`F_u=1`$. Eliminating the module vertices gives $`F_{w_i}=(p_i+r q_i)/a_i`$ and $`U_m+T_mr=0`$. Put

```math
\Lambda_m=\sum_i(-x_{i1}-x_{i2}+p_i^2/a_i),\qquad
g_m=\Lambda_m-U_m^2/T_m.
```

All exceptional equations for the sines, $`a_i,U_m,T_m,p_i+R_mq_i,g_m`$ have nonzero trigonometric-polynomial numerators after clearing denominators. To verify nontriviality, set every core phase to $`\pi/4`$: then $`a_i=3`$, $`U_m=4m/3`$, $`T_m=-m/3`$, $`R_m=4`$, $`F_{w_i}=2\sqrt2`$, and $`g_m=6m`$. Thus the exceptional sets have Lebesgue measure zero.

The pendant equation is $`\tan\theta_0=-g_m`$, with two completions modulo $`2\pi`$. Both have nonzero sine and cosine, and $`F_t=1/\cos\theta_0`$. The pivots of $`M_{\setminus u}`$ are $`-a_1,\ldots,-a_m,T_m,-\cot\theta_0`$, all nonzero. Both completions therefore have a simple eigenvalue and all vertex values nonzero. The invertibility of the core matrix with $`u`$ removed also excludes an additional solution with $`F_u=0`$.

The pendant contributes the same quantity $`\mathbf1_{\{\tan\theta_0<0\}}`$ to $`Q`$ and $`n_+(M_{\setminus u})`$. For module $`i`$, its three sign counts minus $`\mathbf1_{\{a_i<0\}}`$ equal $`1+h_i(r)`$. The last core pivot contributes $`\mathbf1_{\{T_m>0\}}`$. This proves (1) for both completions, so their relative weights are immaterial. The phase-periodic identity applies on the secular manifold by taking positive phase lifts as lengths and $`k=1`$.

## One-module variance and boundary law

Write $`h(r)=\sigma g(r)`$, where $`\sigma=\mathop{\mathrm{sgn}}a`$ and $`g`$ depends only on the cosecants. Simultaneously replacing all three phases by $`\pi-\theta_j`$ modulo $`2\pi`$ fixes their cosecants and negates $`a`$. Thus $`\sigma`$ is a fair sign independent of the cosecant triple, and $`\mathbb Eh(r)=0`$. The sign cases give $`|h(r)|\le1`$.

A uniform phase has law

```math
(\cot\theta,\csc\theta)\overset d=(X,\epsilon\sqrt{1+X^2}),
```

with $`X`$ standard Cauchy and $`\epsilon`$ an independent fair sign. Averaging the eight signs, with $`C_j=|c_j|`$ and $`\rho=|r|`$, gives

```math
\mathbb E[h(r)^2\mid C_1,C_2,C_3]
=\frac14\left(1+\mathbf1_{\{\rho C_3>C_1+C_2\}}
+\mathbf1_{\{\rho C_3<|C_1-C_2|\}}\right).
```

Therefore

```math
v(r):=\mathbb Eh(r)^2
=\frac12-\frac14\mathbb P(|C_1-C_2|<|r|C_3<C_1+C_2). \tag{2}
```

Each $`C_j`$ has continuous law and support $`[1,\infty)`$. For fixed finite nonzero $`r`$, the triangle event and its complement contain nonempty open sets. Hence $`1/4<v(r)<1/2`$. Conditioning on $`C_1,C_2`$ excludes boundary equalities; dominated convergence proves continuity and evenness of $`v`$, and that $`v(r)\to1/2`$ as $`r\to0`$ or $`|r|\to\infty`$. In particular $`v`$ is nonconstant.

Let independent standard Cauchy variables $`X_1,X_2`$ and fair signs $`\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2`$ define

```math
W=\frac{\epsilon_1\sqrt{1+X_1^2}+\epsilon_2\sqrt{1+X_2^2}}
{\sqrt{1+(X_1+X_2)^2}}.
```

Set $`s_0=x_1+x_2`$ and $`\varphi=\theta_3-\arctan s_0`$. Direct substitution gives

```math
\xi^{(2)}=\tan\varphi,\qquad
\xi^{(1)}=\frac{p}{\sqrt{1+s_0^2}}\sec\varphi.
```

Conditionally on the first two phases, $`\varphi`$ is uniform. Its tangent and cosine sign are independent. Symmetry of $`W`$ gives the marginal vector representation

```math
\xi\overset d=(W\sqrt{1+X^2},X),\qquad W\text{ independent of }X.
```

This representation is not substituted for the joint law of $`(h,\xi)`$. Averaging signs and integrating the Cauchy densities, using $`z=x+y`$, yields

```math
\mathbb EW^2
=\frac1{\pi^2}\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{dz}{1+z^2}
\int_{\mathbb R}\left(\frac1{1+x^2}+\frac1{1+(z-x)^2}\right)dx=2.
```

It follows that $`\mathbb E\|\xi\|^{3/4}<\infty`$. The inequality

```math
|\cos(b\sqrt{1+x^2})-\cos(bx)|\le b^2/2
```

follows because $`y\mapsto\cos\sqrt y`$ is $`1/2`$-Lipschitz on $`[0,\infty)`$. Using symmetry of $`W`$, this inequality and the Cauchy characteristic function give, for fixed $`(t,s)`$,

```math
\mathbb E\exp\{i(t\xi^{(1)}+s\xi^{(2)})/m\}
=1-\psi(t,s)/m+O(m^{-2}),\qquad \psi(t,s)=\mathbb E|tW+s|.
```

The second-order remainder is integrable because $`\mathbb EW^2=2`$. Taking $`m`$th powers and applying Lévy's continuity theorem proves

```math
(U_m/m,T_m/m)\Longrightarrow(A,B),\qquad
\mathbb E e^{i(tA+sB)}=e^{-\psi(t,s)}. \tag{3}
```

There is no centering correction. The symmetric variable $`W`$ has positive second moment, so it is nonconstant. The continuous homogeneous function $`\psi`$ is positive away from the origin, hence at least a positive constant times the Euclidean norm. Its exponential is integrable and Fourier inversion gives a continuous density for $`(A,B)`$.

Homogeneity implies $`aZ_1+(1-a)Z_2\overset d=(A,B)`$ for independent copies and $`0<a<1`$, making the support convex. Every nonzero linear projection has a nondegenerate Cauchy law. A proper closed convex support would be contained in a half-space, contradicting this projection property. Thus the planar support is full. In particular $`R=-A/B`$ is finite and nonzero almost surely, has no atoms, and has full support on the real line. Equation (3) gives $`R_m\Longrightarrow R`$.

## Joint limit and uniform moments

Define $`G_m(r)=m^{-1/2}\sum_i h_i(r)`$ for $`r\ne0`$. On each half-line the function of $`\rho=|r|`$ has at most one jump:

```math
h_i(\delta\rho)=\sigma_i\left(b_i^\delta+j_i^\delta
\mathbf1_{\{\tau_i^\delta\le\rho\}}\right),\qquad \delta\in\{-1,1\}.
```

The coefficients depend only on cosecants, $`|b_i^\delta|\le1`$, $`j_i^\delta\in\{-1,0,1\}`$, and $`\tau_i^\delta\in(0,\infty]`$. A finite threshold has no atoms by conditioning on the first two cosecants. Take right-continuous versions. Endogenous evaluation at $`R_m`$ does not encounter a jump almost surely, by the exceptional-set check above.

Split jumps into marks $`\sigma_i j_i^\delta=+1`$ and $`-1`$, assigning threshold infinity to other marks. This writes each process as a centered baseline sum plus the difference of two centered empirical CDF processes. The empirical-CDF functional CLT ([Marckert, arXiv:0710.3296v2, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/0710.3296v2)) and continuous time changes give marginal tightness on compact subintervals of each half-line. The possible mass at infinity is outside these intervals. Joint tightness follows for the four CDF processes and two baselines. The multivariate CLT identifies their joint finite-dimensional limits, giving a continuous centered Gaussian process $`G`$ with

```math
G_m\Longrightarrow G\quad\text{locally in Skorokhod topology},\qquad
\mathbb E[G(r)G(s)]=\mathbb E[h(r)h(s)]. \tag{4}
```

For deterministic $`|d_i|\le1`$, independent fair signs satisfy the exponential-supermartingale estimate

```math
\mathbb P\left(\max_{k\le m}\left|\sum_{i\le k}\sigma_i d_i\right|>z\sqrt m\right)
\le2e^{-z^2/2}\quad(z>0).
```

Condition on the cosecants and order the jumps by threshold. Apply this estimate to the baseline and maximal partial jump sum, then to both half-lines, to obtain

```math
\mathbb P\left(\sup_{r\ne0}|G_m(r)|>x\right)\le8e^{-x^2/8}\quad(x>0). \tag{5}
```

The right-continuous version makes the supremum measurable through rational evaluation. Integrating (5) bounds every fixed positive moment uniformly in $`m`$.

To check independence in the limit, let $`H=\sum_j a_jh(r_j)`$ be any bounded centered linear combination on one actual module. The fractional moment gives $`\mathbb E|e^{i\zeta\cdot\xi/m}-1|=O(m^{-3/4})`$. Expand only $`e^{iH/\sqrt m}`$ to second order. The mixed linear term is $`O(m^{-5/4})=o(m^{-1})`$, and dominated convergence treats the quadratic term. Therefore

```math
\mathbb E e^{i\zeta\cdot\xi/m+iH/\sqrt m}
=1-\frac{\psi(\zeta)+\mathbb EH^2/2}{m}+o(m^{-1}).
```

Taking powers identifies the product characteristic function. Together with tightness and a countable determining set for continuous paths, this proves

```math
(G_m,U_m/m,T_m/m)\Longrightarrow(G,A,B),\qquad G\text{ independent of }(A,B).
```

Localize to $`L^{-1}\le|R_m|\le L`$, apply the continuous mapping theorem, and let $`L\to\infty`$. Since $`R`$ is finite and nonzero almost surely,

```math
G_m(R_m)\Longrightarrow G(R)\overset d=Z\sqrt{v(R)},
```

where $`Z`$ is standard normal and independent of $`R`$.

## Transfer to the prescribed lengths and failure of Gaussian universality

Put $`V=v(R)`$ and $`a_*=\mathbb EV`$. Continuity, nonconstancy of $`v`$, and full support of $`R`$ imply $`\mathop{\mathrm{Var}}(V)>0`$; also $`1/4<V<1/2`$ almost surely. By (1), (5), and uniform integrability,

```math
\widetilde X_m:=\frac{\widetilde S_m-\beta_m/2}{\sqrt m}
\Longrightarrow Z\sqrt V,\qquad
\mathbb E\widetilde X_m^2\to a_*,\qquad
\mathbb E\widetilde X_m^4\to3\mathbb EV^2.
```

The core-length fraction satisfies $`\omega_m\le3/(m^5+3)`$: every core square root is smaller than the pendant prime's square root, and $`x/(x+\ell_0)`$ is increasing in $`x`$. The spectral mixture is

```math
\mathcal L(S_m)=(1-\omega_m)\mathcal L(\widetilde S_m)+\omega_m\mathcal R_m
```

for a probability law $`\mathcal R_m`$. Its total-variation error from the pendant law is at most $`\omega_m`$. All surplus laws have center $`\beta_m/2`$ and support $`[0,\beta_m]`$; existence and symmetry also follow from [Alon–Band–Berkolaiko, arXiv:1709.10413v2, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1709.10413v2). For $`X_m=(S_m-\beta_m/2)/\sqrt m`$ and $`p=2,4`$,

```math
\left|\mathbb E|X_m|^p-\mathbb E|\widetilde X_m|^p\right|
\le\omega_m\left(\frac{\beta_m}{2\sqrt m}\right)^p
=O(m^{p/2-5})\longrightarrow0.
```

It follows that

```math
\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{Var}}(S_m)}{\beta_m}\longrightarrow\frac{a_*}{2}\in(1/8,1/4),\qquad
\frac{S_m-\beta_m/2}{\sqrt{\mathop{\mathrm{Var}}(S_m)}}
\Longrightarrow Z\sqrt{V/a_*}.
```

For every nonzero $`t`$, strict Jensen inequality, applied to the nonconstant bounded variable $`V`$, gives

```math
\mathbb E e^{-t^2V/(2a_*)}>e^{-t^2/2}.
```

Thus the limit is not standard Gaussian. Its fourth moment is $`3\mathbb EV^2/a_*^2>3`$, and the standardized fourth moments of $`S_m`$ converge to it. This disproves unrestricted Gaussian universality while leaving the separate universal linear-variance question open.

The construction has growing hub degrees and a dominant pendant length. It does not settle bounded-degree or comparable-length variants, or a joint graph-size/spectral-window limit.
