## Independent proof audit

1. **Entire histories for arbitrary measures.** For compactly supported mu, H(s,x)=integral exp(-zx/2+z2 s/4) dmu is finite, strictly positive and smooth on every compact set of real (s,x). Differentiation under the integral is justified by the bounded support. H_s=H_xx, and direct logarithmic differentiation gives the viscous Burgers equation for U=-2H_x/H. U is a positive weighted average of z, so it stays in [alpha,beta] for every real time, including each negative patch time. At zero it equals the target profile. No finite-support approximation or density assumption on mu is used.

2. **Cole-Hopf solution for bounded data.** The normalized primitive exponential h_g grows at most exp(M|y|/2), which is dominated by the heat Gaussian together with all needed positive-time derivatives. Since h_g'=-g h_g/2 almost everywhere, integration by parts gives the stated weighted-average representation of S_t g. This preserves the value interval. The heat equation gives the nonlinear PDE, and the local approximate-identity argument gives the initial trace at Lebesgue points and in local L1. The standard bounded solution identified in the cited source is therefore the one being constructed.

3. **Uniform locality, including denominators.** Equality of g and h on [-R,R] gives equality of their normalized exponentials there because both primitives are based at zero. Outside that interval their difference is uniformly dominated by twice exp(M|y|/2), independently of all other patches. The heat-kernel and first-derivative tails tend to zero uniformly on the observation compact set for a fixed time. Recomputed the positive denominator lower bound by integration over [-1,1]; its displayed constants are valid. The numerator derivative has a finite uniform upper bound by the same Gaussian domination. The quotient estimate is thus uniform over arbitrary exterior data. This controls the infinite collection of all other patches, rather than just finitely many nearby ones.

4. **Exact evolution of the backward history.** The normalized Cole-Hopf datum of U(-k,.) is H(-k,x)/H(-k,0). Convolution of exp(-zx/2) with G_k multiplies it by exp(z2 k/4). Tonelli applies to the positive H integral and cancels the negative-time factor, giving S_k U(-k,.)=phi_mu exactly. This step does not assume an ill-posed generic backward evolution; the special entire history is explicit.

5. **One globally defined initial datum.** At stage k, the radius is selected after the finite time k and tolerance 1/k, and depends only on the common value bound and the selected observation window. All radii are finite. Centers can then be chosen successively with positive gaps, producing disjoint intervals with no finite accumulation. The countable piecewise definition is measurable and retains the prescribed bounds. On each translated patch it agrees exactly with the corresponding entire history. Translation invariance of the Cole-Hopf solution follows because a translated primitive differs only by a positive constant factor, which cancels in its logarithmic derivative. The locality lemma now proves the claimed error simultaneously for every k after all patches are present. No assertion of finite propagation speed or interchange of a time limit with a radius limit is needed.

6. **Locally uniform convergence.** For a fixed compact interval [-L,L], every k>=L gives the estimate on that interval, and 1/k tends to zero. Since t_k=k tends to infinity, this is exactly the desired translated late-time limit, even when the measure has a continuous component or infinitely many atoms.

7. **Stronger universal-datum corollary.** Independently checked the additional claim. Probability measures on the fixed compact interval form a compact metrizable space. Repeated finite blocks from a countable dense family give a sequence in which every measure is a subsequential limit along indices tending to infinity. The same locality bound works with the same common M for the stage-dependent measures. Weak convergence of measures makes both profile integrals converge uniformly on compact x-intervals: compact z-support gives equicontinuity, and the denominator is uniformly at least exp(-M L/2) on [-L,L]. The quotient therefore converges there. The diagonal/subsequence argument realizes every target profile from one datum, as claimed.
